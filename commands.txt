4. What license_type actually represents
A broad sample of license_type/license_type_desc pairs confirms the table mixes multiple distinct categories under one field 4:
Genuine regulatory/product licences: e.g., 01/02/03/04 – Life insurance licence variants, 21–24 – General insurance licence variants, 31 – MPF (Pension), 3M – Macau Pension License, 5B – Investment advisory.
Training and examination records: e.g., 8D – New Agent Training, 8E – PA/EDP Follow Up Training, 60/61 – VUL classroom/examination, 50–55 – IL (various training/certification paths), 64 – Master e-Test.
Compliance/certification/regulatory-process items: e.g., 84 – FATCA, 88 – Common Report Standard (CRS), 85/32 – Business Ethics eExercise, 87 – Contracting (HKFI), 6A – Universal Foreigner Verification.
Product eligibility / sales-authorization flags: e.g., 80 – Eligible Agent to sell Asset Accumulator Plan, 63 – Eligible Agent to sell China Policy, 82 – CIES.
Penalty / suspension flags recorded in the same field: e.g., 71 – "Penalty - not allow to sell Universal Life Products," 73 – "Suspend selling AMR," 76 – "Suspend selling wealth preserver."
Verified conclusion: The table combines genuine regulatory licences, internal training/certification records, compliance attestations (FATCA/CRS/Business Ethics), and sales-eligibility/penalty flags under a single license_type taxonomy. No metadata field distinguishes these categories explicitly (there is no "category" or "licence class" column), so any classification into "licence" vs. "training" vs. "certification" vs. "restriction" would have to be done manually per code based on the description text — this is not automatically derivable and should not be assumed without a controlled mapping exercise. Consequently, counting "licences" using the full license_type universe risks conflating regulatory authorizations with training completions and penalty markers; any business metric should clarify which subset of types it intends to include.

6. Date boundary risk with CURRENT_DATE()
Verified facts:
license_eff_date and license_exp_date carry no time-of-day component across all 4,804,207 records checked — both fields are always midnight-aligned (eff_has_time_component = 0, exp_has_time_component = 0) 6. This means comparing these timestamp columns directly against CURRENT_DATE() does not risk truncation errors from a non-zero time component.
However, there genuinely are records whose license_exp_date equals today's date (5 PD-TR, 14 Agency records expire exactly today) and none effective exactly today in the current snapshot 7. Using a strict < or > comparison at the boundary (e.g., license_exp_date > CURRENT_DATE()) would incorrectly exclude licences that are valid through the entirety of today.
Recommendation: The existing rule license_eff_date <= CURRENT_DATE() AND license_exp_date >= CURRENT_DATE() is the correct formulation for "valid for the full current calendar day" — it correctly includes records starting or expiring today, since both dates are clean calendar dates with no time component. This rule should continue to be used as-is; no change is needed, but analysts should avoid substituting strict </> operators at the boundary, which would wrongly exclude today's starting/expiring licences.
7. Is the 2099 expiry convention documented in metadata?
Verified: No. No column description, lookup table reference, or other metadata field in either gold_agent_license or gold_agent_master states that expiry dates on or after 2099-01-01 represent "non-expiring" licences. This convention was supplied to this analysis as an external business rule from prior instructions, not derived from or confirmed by the table metadata itself.
