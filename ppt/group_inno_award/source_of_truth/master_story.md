## 3. Problem statement (form field 7)

**Core problem — as AI grows, the burden of navigating it can fall on the person. [OBSERVATION for back office; ASSUMPTION for front office — see ledger 1, 28.]**

AIA is building more AI capabilities, including valuable specialist AI such as Alfred **[SUPPLIED]**. Each capability can make a task smarter. But from what Harry observes, two things still fall on the person:

1. **The digital legwork around the outcome.** Finding data, opening systems, reading documents, searching emails and chats, checking information, analysing, combining outputs and drafting the result. *Today, the employee is the connector.* **[OBSERVATION]**
2. **Navigating the AI landscape.** Knowing which AI capabilities exist, which fits which task, how to use each one, and how to move information between them. As more capabilities arrive, people can be asked to understand more of the landscape, not less. **[OBSERVATION / ASSUMPTION — no counts or tool names cited]**

**The same pattern is likely to apply in the front office.** An agent preparing for a customer meeting may also be gathering information across several sources before the conversation. **[ASSUMPTION — Harry works in the back office and has not observed agents' workflows; to validate with Distribution before any front-office work.]**

**Supporting context [BENCHMARK].** McKinsey Global Institute, *The social economy* (2012): interaction workers spend nearly 20% of the workweek looking for internal information or tracking down colleagues who can help with specific tasks. Used only as evidence that information-seeking is a large category of work; not described as time "lost", does not cover the full chain, and predates today's AI tools. McKinsey (2025): nearly eight in ten companies use gen AI but about as many report no significant bottom-line impact (the "gen AI paradox") — context for why the experience of using AI matters, not evidence about AIA.

**Scale.** Outward: "a large back-office population across many functions" and, as a vision, AIA's agents. No employee or agent counts are asserted. **[ASSUMPTION]** Harry's internal working estimate for the back office (on the order of a couple thousand) remains internal only.

**Wording discipline:** do not say the chain or the landscape "gets longer/more complex" as a fact; say people "can be asked to" or "may be left to". Do not present effort or navigation burden as measured.

## 4. The solution — what Riley is (form field 8)

**Riley is an experience, not a platform.** The person asks Riley for an outcome in plain language. Riley shows its plan, does the digital work in between using only the approved AI, data and systems that person is permitted to use, and returns finished work for review. The person keeps judgment, review and final approval.

**The person does not need to:** find every AI tool · learn every new AI tool · decide which AI capability to use · move information manually between tools · coordinate several AI tools themselves.

**How Riley fits with AIA's AI — underneath / Riley / around** (replaces brain/hands/rules outwardly):
- **Underneath — AIA's AI and systems.** Enterprise AI (Copilot or another approved enterprise AI platform) provides the intelligence to understand, reason, plan and draft. Specialist AI built by AIA teams, such as Alfred, could provide domain expertise. AIA data, documents and systems provide the AIA-specific knowledge. Microsoft 365 and approved external sources are standard inputs. *AIA keeps building better AI underneath.*
- **Riley — the coworker experience.** One consistent way for an employee or agent to ask for an outcome, see the plan, and get finished work back. Delivered where people already work: Microsoft Teams for employees; for agents, the channel is to be decided with Distribution **[ASSUMPTION, ledger 30]**.
- **Around — AIA's rules.** The person's own permissions, approval before final action, an appropriate record of the work, governed learning, and for any future front-office use, AIA's suitability, conduct, privacy and customer-communication controls (Section 13).

**How the work runs (unchanged mechanics, reframed):**
1. The person describes the outcome, in natural language.
2. Riley works out what the outcome needs, using only what that person is authorized to use.
3. Riley plans, carries out the intermediate steps (finding, reading, checking, analysing, combining, drafting) and assembles finished work.
4. **Approval model:** Riley may plan and execute intermediate steps itself, but the person reviews, corrects and approves the output **before anything is finalized, sent to anyone, or entered into a business system**. (*Review before final action* — never "human in the loop at every step".)
5. **Traceable work:** an appropriate record of the request, capabilities used, output, approval and final action.
6. **Feedback loop:** corrections and approvals, under governance, improve how Riley handles AIA-specific work.

**Wording discipline (Revision 9):** never call Riley an orchestration platform, an orchestration layer, an agent platform, a hub or a router. Avoid "orchestrate" and "route" outwardly. Keep the person and the outcome as the subject of sentences, not tools. The orchestration underneath may be described once, plainly ("Riley uses the approved capabilities available to that person"), never as the innovation.

**Long-term architecture vs Phase 1:** everything "underneath" describes where Riley can go. Phase 1 uses only the minimum capabilities one back-office workflow needs (Section 11).

## 5. Back-office vision and illustrative example [ILLUSTRATIVE]

**Back-office vision [VISION].** An employee asks Riley for outcomes such as: prepare a management review · analyse a control issue · research a business question · compare documents · prepare a meeting pack · draft a report · review materials · combine information across approved sources. Riley does the digital legwork in between and returns work for review.

**Detailed example — the quarterly complaints review.** Not the committed first pilot: the first live workflow is chosen in Phase 0, and a lower-sensitivity workflow may be selected. No internal system names, tool names or data sources are invented.

1. **The employee asks Riley in Teams:** *"Prepare the quarterly complaints review for Thursday's management meeting: top issues versus last quarter, likely drivers, what is already being done, and draft slides."*
2. **Riley shows its plan**, using only what this employee is permitted to access.
3. **Riley pulls complaint records** for this quarter and the previous quarter.
4. **Riley groups the complaints into themes**, using an approved AI capability where one exists.
5. **Riley checks relevant internal documents**, such as the previous review and procedures.
6. **Riley checks relevant Outlook and Teams updates** available to that employee.
7. **Riley checks approved external sources** for relevant industry or regulatory context.
8. **Riley analyses and compares** the information, drafts the management summary and prepares the presentation.
9. **The employee reviews, edits and approves.** Nothing is finalized, sent or entered into a business system before approval.

**Revision 9 presentation change:** describe the steps as the work Riley does for the outcome, not as a list of tools being routed. The employee never had to choose which AI capability grouped the complaints. Step counts may be shown; no timings.

**Before / after (illustrative, not measured):** without Riley, the employee carries out every step and decides which tools to use; with Riley, the employee sets the outcome (step 1) and reviews and approves (step 9).

## 6. Front-office vision and illustrative example [VISION / ILLUSTRATIVE — not in Phases 0–2]

**Front-office vision [VISION].** An AIA agent preparing to meet a potential customer asks Riley for an outcome: *"Help me prepare for this customer meeting."*

Subject to permissions, product rules, privacy, compliance and suitability controls, Riley could potentially:
- use customer information the agent is already permitted to access
- use AIA product information
- use approved internal knowledge
- use permitted external information for general context (such as relevant market or product-category context) — **not** profiling the individual beyond what AIA's privacy rules permit
- identify relevant considerations for the conversation
- prepare a meeting brief
- help structure possible discussion points
- help draft follow-up materials for the agent to review

**Hard boundaries:**
- **The agent remains responsible** for judgment, suitability, advice and every customer communication.
- **Riley does not decide or recommend what product a customer should buy**, and never communicates with a customer directly.
- Follow-up materials are drafts for the agent; anything sent to a customer passes the agent's review and AIA's existing customer-communication and compliance requirements.
- Front-office use is **future potential**. It needs Distribution, Compliance, Legal and Privacy involvement before any design or pilot (Section 13).

**Why it matters:** the same principle — ask for the outcome, not the tool — applies whether someone is serving a customer or running the business. That is what makes Riley an enterprise-wide experience, not an IT integration project.

## 7. Relationship with AIA's existing and emerging AI

**Position:** Riley builds on AIA's AI. It does not replace, compete with or duplicate it.

- **Not** a new AI model, agent technology or orchestration platform. **Not** a competitor to Copilot. **Not** a replacement for Alfred or any specialist AI. **Not** a claim that AIA lacks an AI strategy.
- **Enterprise AI (e.g., Copilot)** could be part of the intelligence underneath Riley. *Copilot could be the engine.* The platform choice is a build decision, not the innovation.
- **Specialist AI (e.g., Alfred)** could become one of the capabilities Riley uses, subject to technical and governance feasibility.
- **AIA systems and data** form Riley's AIA-specific capabilities.
- **Riley** is the consistent employee- and agent-facing experience around those capabilities.

**Outward lines:** *"AIA can keep building better AI capabilities underneath. Riley gives employees and agents one consistent way to benefit from them."* · *"The more AI AIA builds, the simpler the experience should become."*

**If the platform gets better — or if Group builds agents.** As enterprise AI and AIA's own agent capabilities improve, Riley improves, because they sit underneath it. AIA still has to decide what its people experience: how they ask, what they see, what they approve, what is recorded, and which capabilities each person can use. That experience is Riley. **[HYPOTHESIS / positioning]**

### Alfred [SUPPLIED]
AIA Group Internal Audit (GIA) has developed an internal GenAI web platform called Alfred that supports internal auditors across the audit lifecycle. As described by Harry, it can: act as an AI subject-matter expert using maintained FAQ and GIA methodology repositories; give consistent responses based on internal methodology; review draft reports and workpapers against templates, standards, processes and requirements; perform logic review and reasoning; review templates; support issue rating and recommendations; assist with audit testing; ingest documents for analysis; identify key control objectives and control activities; help draft and check control design effectiveness; produce structured workpapers; compare uploaded documents against evolving regulatory guidance and best practices; identify gaps; and recommend updates to internal standards. No other Alfred functionality may be claimed.

**Role in the story (Revision 9):** Alfred is evidence that AIA is already building valuable specialist AI. **Alfred is not the problem.** *Alfred shows what specialist AI can do for one domain. Riley is the broader employee experience that could allow capabilities like Alfred to contribute to a wider outcome.* **[HYPOTHESIS, subject to technical and governance feasibility]**

**Wording discipline:** always "a capability like Alfred" or "Alfred could potentially become one of the capabilities Riley can use, subject to technical and governance feasibility". Never claim Riley can connect to Alfred today, that Alfred exposes a software interface, any time Alfred saves, that Alfred's users struggle, that Alfred is inadequate, or that GIA endorses Riley.

### The three waves — supporting evidence, not the definition [positioning; wave 3 is a HYPOTHESIS]
1. **Manual work** — the person finds, reads, checks, compares and drafts everything.
2. **Specialist AI, such as Alfred** — AI does much more of the specialist task. The person still chooses the tool, provides the inputs, and carries its output into the rest of the work.
3. **Delegating the outcome, with Riley** — the person asks for the outcome; Riley uses the approved capabilities available, which could include one like Alfred; the person reviews and decides.

**Use:** the waves explain *why Riley becomes more useful as AIA builds more AI*. They do not define Riley and do not make "AI using AI" the innovation.

**External context [BENCHMARK, context only]:** task-level AI gains are well documented (Dell'Acqua et al., HBS/BCG, 2023: consultants using GPT-4 on tasks inside AI's capability frontier completed 12.2% more tasks, 25.1% faster, at over 40% higher quality, and did worse outside it); McKinsey (2025) "gen AI paradox" (Section 3); HBR (2022) found workers toggle between applications about 1,200 times a day, just under four hours a week reorienting. No credible benchmark exists for training or switching time saved by one interface.

**What this does not claim:** connections are not free or automatic. Each needs technical integration and governance review, and adds value only if the capability is genuinely useful. Value does not grow in a straight line or by a formula. **[HYPOTHESIS]**

## 8. Creativity (form field 13)

**What is new — the true innovation:**
1. **Outcome, not tool.** The unit of interaction is the outcome the person needs, not the AI tool they must choose. *Ask for the outcome, not the tool.*
2. **A principle for AIA's AI growth.** *As AIA builds more AI, our people should need to understand less of it, not more.* Riley is designed so each new capability underneath makes the experience more useful without making it more complicated.
3. **One consistent coworker across front and back office.** The same experience whether someone is serving a customer or running the business, adapted by permissions and controls.
4. **Human judgment designed in.** The division of work is explicit: Riley does the digital legwork; the person owns the outcome, the judgment and the approval.

**No longer claimed as innovative (Revision 9):** agent orchestration · "AI using AI" or connecting AI tools · multi-step agent execution as a technology · being a platform or layer · the multiplier as a mechanism · whole-outcome execution as a technical capability (general agents are moving this way) · Teams chat · Microsoft 365 or web access · AIA-specific capabilities as a differentiator by themselves (Group's agents could have those too).

**The honest framing (outward):** *Riley does not propose new AI technology. It proposes what AIA's people should experience as AIA's AI grows.* Saying this first removes the "copied from Group" objection and makes the originality clear: a point of view and a design, not a claim on the engine.

**Supporting contrast:** most AI helps a person do a step; Riley is designed around delegating an outcome. The three waves (Section 7) show why this becomes more valuable as AIA builds more AI.

## 9. Enduring value (award theme: "Innovation for Enduring Value")

Riley is designed so its usefulness grows while the experience stays simple:
1. **Every useful capability AIA adds underneath** can expand what people can get done, without a new tool to learn.
2. **Employee and agent feedback** keeps improving how Riley supports AIA-specific work.
3. **The same coworker expands** into more workflows, more teams and, in future, the front office.
4. **AIA's existing and future AI investments become easier to benefit from**, not harder.

## 10. Impact (form field 12)

**Headline proposition:** *A digital execution partner for every employee and agent.* (Evolves Revision 7's "The prize isn't an hour saved. It's a digital execution partner for every employee.", which may remain as a supporting line with "and agent" added.)

### Level 1 — Near-term pilot impact (measured, not assumed) — unchanged scope

Measured on the real Phase 1 back-office team against the Phase 0 baseline:

| Metric | What it measures | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|

## 13. Governance — designed for an insurer

Conceptual and practical. No specific memory, logging or training architecture is committed to.

**For all use (unchanged from Revision 8):**
1. **The person's own access only.** Riley acts with the access the requesting person is already authorized to use and never bypasses existing permissions, including Outlook, Teams and file content, only through approved enterprise controls.
2. **Data stays under AIA controls.** Sensitive and customer data remain subject to AIA's existing data, privacy and information-security controls. External information comes only from approved sources.
3. **Approval before final action.** Riley may plan and execute intermediate steps, but the person reviews and approves the output before anything is finalized, sent to anyone, or entered into a business system. The person remains accountable.
4. **Traceable and reviewable.** An appropriate record of the request, capabilities used, output, approval and final action, under AIA's existing policies, confirmed in Phase 0.
5. **Governed learning.** What feedback is captured, who can see it, and how improvements are reviewed are agreed with risk and IT in Phase 0.
6. **Independent quality checks** during the pilot.

**Additional controls before any front-office use (new in Revision 9) [design requirements, to be defined with Distribution, Compliance, Legal and Privacy]:**
7. **Advice and suitability stay with the agent.** Riley prepares and drafts; it does not recommend products, assess suitability or make any customer decision.
8. **No direct customer contact.** Riley never communicates with a customer. Every customer-facing material is reviewed by the agent and passes AIA's existing customer-communication and compliance requirements.
9. **Customer data only as permitted.** Only customer information the agent is already permitted to access, used within AIA's privacy policies and the purposes customers have agreed to.
10. **Limited external research.** Approved sources for general context only; no profiling of individuals beyond what AIA's privacy rules permit.
11. **Product information from approved sources only**, so briefs reflect current, approved product rules.

## 14. Scalability (form field 14)

- **Three directions of scale:** more people (back-office employees first, then, as a vision, agents and front-line colleagues), more workflows, and more capabilities underneath.
- **Near-term:** a large back-office population across many functions is in scope before any front-office or cross-market expansion.
- **Front office [VISION]:** the same experience extends to agents once the front-office controls (Section 13) are designed and approved. Agents may need a different channel and access model **[ASSUMPTION]**.
- **Mechanism:** each approved capability AIA adds underneath can become available to permitted people through the same coworker, with no new tool to learn **[HYPOTHESIS, tested in Phase 2]**.
- **Cross-market:** reuse the core Riley experience; each market adds its own integrations, data controls, governance, regulatory and process configuration. Not a near-free replication.
- **Vision:** one AI coworker for every AIA employee and agent **[VISION, not a forecast]**.

## 15. Feasibility (form field 15)

- Big vision, narrow pilot: one back-office team, one workflow, minimum capabilities, gated on measured results. Front office is future potential only.
- Built on AIA's existing and emerging AI capabilities; no new model or agent technology. Platform left open.
- Delivered in Microsoft Teams for employees, lowering adoption friction; trust still has to be earned through reliable results. Channel for agents to be decided later.
- Governance designed in from the start (Section 13), with additional front-office controls required before any agent use.
- Phase 1 does not depend on connecting any specialist capability such as Alfred.
