## 0. Anchor lines

**Definition (the line judges should remember):**
> **Riley is AIA's AI coworker: you give it an outcome, and it uses the same approved data, systems and tools you would use to do the work in between, then brings back finished work for you to approve.**

Short form: *You own the outcome. Riley does the digital legwork.*

**Impact proposition:**
> **The prize isn't an hour saved. It's a digital execution partner for every employee.**

**Supporting problem insight (demoted from the definition in Revision 7):** *Today, the employee is the connector. Riley becomes the connector.* Use it to describe the problem, not to define Riley.

**Brain + hands + AIA's rules [positioning]:**
- **Brain — enterprise AI.** Copilot or another approved enterprise AI platform provides the intelligence to understand requests, reason, plan and draft. Riley does not need its own model and does not compete with Copilot. *Copilot could be the engine.*
- **Hands — everything Riley is approved to use to do the work.** AIA data, AIA documents, AIA systems, internal AI tools built by AIA teams, Microsoft 365 (such as Outlook, Teams and files) and approved external information. The hands that are unique to AIA — **AIA data, AIA systems and AIA's internal AI tools** — are where Riley is distinctive; Microsoft 365 and web access are standard equipment, not the innovation.
- **AIA's rules — around the coworker.** Employee permissions, approval before final action, an appropriate record of the work, and governed learning.
- **Coworker — Riley.** Brain + hands + AIA's rules, through one employee-facing experience in Teams. The employee interacts with Riley, not with each tool.

**Long-term vision, in full [VISION].** Every AIA employee — not only a pilot team, not only the back office — has access to Riley on demand. Employees hand Riley the outcome; Riley does much of the digital execution in between — finding and pulling data, reading documents, checking updates, researching approved sources, analysing, combining and drafting — and returns finished work for review. Employees spend more of their effort on judgment, decisions, review, customer understanding, problem-solving and innovation. As AIA connects more useful capabilities, Riley can absorb more of the digital work through the same coworker. Human accountability does not change: the employee owns the outcome and approves before anything is final, and Riley works within the employee's permissions and AIA's governance.

## 2. Problem statement (form field 7)

**Primary problem — the employee does the digital legwork. [OBSERVATION — based on Harry's direct observation as an AIA employee. Not quantitatively proven; outward wording must present it as an observation ("from what I observe…"). No names, counts, timings or specific internal tools may be cited or invented.]**

Much of an employee's effort goes into the digital work around the actual outcome: finding data, opening systems, reading documents, searching emails and chats, checking external information, analysing, combining outputs and drafting the final result. The employee is the one who joins all of this together — *today, the employee is the connector.*

AI tools can help with individual steps — a summary here, a draft there — but the employee can still be left coordinating the whole chain. As AIA adds more systems and AI capabilities, including tools built by different teams, employees may still be left coordinating work across them by hand. **[OBSERVATION]** Another single-step tool does not remove that coordination burden.

**Supporting context [BENCHMARK].** McKinsey Global Institute, *The social economy* (2012): interaction workers (high-skill knowledge workers) spend nearly 20% of the workweek looking for internal information or tracking down colleagues who can help with specific tasks. Used only as evidence that information-seeking is a large, real category of work. It is **not** described as time "lost", it does **not** cover the full chain of digital execution, and it predates today's AI tools.

**Scale.** Outward-facing wording: "a large back-office population across many functions." Harry's internal working estimate (on the order of a couple thousand employees) is retained as internal context only and is not asserted in the submission. **[ASSUMPTION]**

**Wording discipline:** do not say the chain "gets longer" as a fact; do not present fragmentation or effort as measured.

## 3. Illustrative end-to-end example — not the definition of Riley [ILLUSTRATIVE]

The complaints review is the main illustration because it shows Riley doing a meaningful chain of digital work across several kinds of capability. It is **not** the committed first pilot: the first live workflow is chosen in Phase 0, and a lower-sensitivity workflow may be selected. No internal system names, tool names or data sources are invented.

1. **The employee asks Riley in Teams:** *"Prepare the quarterly complaints review for Thursday's management meeting: top issues versus last quarter, likely drivers, what is already being done, and draft slides."*
2. **Riley shows its plan**, using only what this employee is permitted to access.
3. **Riley pulls complaint records** for this quarter and the previous quarter (AIA data).
4. **Riley uses an internal AI capability** to group the complaints into themes (an example of a tool built by an AIA team).
5. **Riley checks relevant internal documents**, such as the previous review and procedures.
6. **Riley checks relevant Outlook and Teams updates** available to that employee.
7. **Riley checks approved external sources** for relevant industry or regulatory context.
8. **Riley analyses and compares** the information, drafts the management summary and prepares the presentation.
9. **The employee reviews, edits and approves.** Nothing is finalized, sent or entered into a business system before approval.

**Before / after (illustrative, not measured):** without Riley, the employee carries out every step by hand across several tools; with Riley, the employee sets the outcome (step 1) and reviews and approves the result (step 9), while Riley does the digital execution in between. Step counts may be shown as part of the illustration; no timings may be attached.

The same pattern — research, analysis, reporting, coordination, drafting — applies across back-office functions. Riley is a broad employee capability, not complaints automation.

## 4. How Riley works (form field 8, core mechanics)

1. The employee describes the outcome they want, in natural language, in Microsoft Teams.
2. Riley works out what the task needs, drawing only on what the employee is authorized to use: AIA data and documents; approved AIA systems; internal AI tools built by AIA teams; Microsoft 365 information such as relevant Outlook and Teams updates; approved external information sources; and other approved enterprise capabilities.
3. Riley plans the work, carries out the intermediate steps (finding, reading, checking, analysing, combining, drafting) and assembles a finished piece of work.
4. **Approval model:** during the pilot, Riley may plan and execute intermediate steps itself, but the employee reviews, corrects and approves the output **before anything is finalized, sent to anyone, or entered into a business system**. The employee remains accountable for the result. (*Review before final action* — never described as "human in the loop at every step".)
5. **Traceable work:** Riley keeps an appropriate record of each piece of work — the request, the systems or capabilities used, the output, the employee's approval, and the final action where relevant (Section 12).
6. **Feedback loop:** corrections, approvals and feedback, captured under governance (Section 12), improve how Riley handles AIA-specific work: understanding requests, choosing the right capabilities, following business processes and avoiding repeated mistakes.

**Long-term architecture vs Phase 1:** the full set of hands describes where Riley can go. Phase 1 uses only the minimum capabilities needed to demonstrate one meaningful workflow (Section 10).

## 5. Differentiation — "Isn't this just Copilot?"

**Position:** Riley does not compete with Copilot or any enterprise AI platform, and does not need its own model. *Copilot could be the engine* — Copilot Studio, another approved enterprise platform, or future technology. The platform choice is a build decision, not the innovation. Because Riley's broader hands include Microsoft 365, the distinction must never rest on Outlook, Teams or web access, which general assistants also offer. It rests on three things:

1. **AIA-specific hands** — AIA data, AIA systems and AI tools built by AIA teams, which a general assistant does not arrive with.
2. **Whole-outcome execution** — Riley carries out the chain of digital work behind an outcome and returns finished work, not an isolated summary or draft.
3. **AIA's rules** — employee permissions, approval before final action, traceability and governed learning applied to every piece of work.

**If the platform gets better.** As enterprise AI gets smarter — including at routing work between tools — Riley gets smarter too. AIA still has to decide which of its data, systems and AI tools Riley can use, for whom, with what approval and records, and how feedback and learning are governed. That AIA-specific coworker is Riley. A more capable platform makes Riley a more capable coworker, not an irrelevant one. **[HYPOTHESIS / positioning]**

**Removed claim (2026-09-21, unchanged):** "no existing AIA tool unifies internal AI capabilities this way today" — cannot be proven across AIA.

## 6. Why Riley becomes more capable over time (the multiplier — supporting role since Revision 7)

The multiplier is **not** the definition of Riley. It is one reason Riley's value can compound.

**Logic:** Riley works with the capabilities it is approved to use. Every useful capability AIA connects — a system, a data source, an internal AI tool, or another approved enterprise service — can expand what Riley can do through the same coworker experience, for employees permitted to use it. Without a common coworker, each new capability risks becoming another separate thing for employees to find, learn and coordinate by hand.

**Outward wording:** *"Every useful capability AIA connects can expand what Riley can do through the same coworker experience."*

**What this does not claim:** connections are not free or automatic. Each needs technical integration and governance review, and adds value only if the capability is genuinely useful to Riley's users. Value does not grow in a straight line or by a formula. **[HYPOTHESIS]**

**How it is tested:** Phase 2 extends Riley with another approved capability — an AI tool built by another AIA team is the strongest example — and a second workflow or team (Section 10).

## 7. Enduring value (award theme: "Innovation for Enduring Value")

Riley is designed so its usefulness can grow, rather than delivering a one-time productivity gain:
1. **Every useful capability connected** can expand what the same coworker can do.
2. **Employee feedback keeps improving** how Riley supports AIA-specific work.
3. **The same coworker expands into more workflows and teams**, without a new tool each time.
4. **Existing and future AI investments become easier for employees to use**, instead of harder.

Use the theme phrase where it naturally fits (Scalability, closing lines), not everywhere.

## 8. Double Employee Value

Riley absorbs more of the digital execution — searching, switching, copying, coordinating, analysing and drafting — so employees can put more of their effort into judgment, decision-making, review, customer understanding, problem-solving and innovation. The framing is **greater employee execution capacity and released capacity for higher-value work.** No headcount, FTE or specific corporate KPI claims are made.

## 9. Impact (form field 12)

**Headline proposition:** *The prize isn't an hour saved. It's a digital execution partner for every employee.* Riley aims to increase how much useful, finished work an employee can complete by absorbing more of the digital execution around it.

Impact is presented on **two levels**.

### Level 1 — Near-term pilot impact (measured, not assumed)

Measured on the real Phase 1 team against the Phase 0 baseline:

| Metric | What it measures | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Active employee effort per completed task | Time the employee actually spends working the task, before vs. after Riley | Direct test of reduced manual execution |
| End-to-end completion time | Elapsed time from request to finished, approved output | Speed of the outcome, not just effort |
| Manual steps / systems touched | Steps and systems previously handled manually vs. now handled by Riley | Direct test of the digital-legwork problem |
| Rework rate | Share of Riley's outputs needing significant correction before approval | Quality, and how fast Riley improves via feedback |
| Quality | Accuracy/completeness vs. a human-only baseline — rated by the requesting employee **and** confirmed through periodic independent sample reviews by an appropriate business reviewer or subject-matter expert | Makes the quality result credible, not self-assessed |
| Adoption and repeat use | Share of eligible pilot employees using Riley weekly/monthly; repeat use | Evidence employees trust it and choose it |
| **Completed outputs per employee** (new, Revision 7) | Finished, approved outputs per pilot employee over a period, before vs. after Riley | Tests whether Riley increases execution capacity, not only reduces time |

### Level 2 — Long-term strategic impact [VISION / HYPOTHESIS]

- **Greater employee execution capacity:** one employee can supervise significantly more digital work because Riley performs much more of the execution. **[VISION]**
- **More finished work through the same employee**, with more of their effort reserved for judgment, relationships, decisions, review and innovation. **[VISION]**
- **Riley can absorb more digital execution as more capabilities are connected** (Section 6). **[HYPOTHESIS]**
- **Long-term ambition, stated as a question to test:** *"Can one employee, supported by Riley, complete substantially more finished work than manual digital execution allows?"* **[HYPOTHESIS]**

**Hard wording rules:** do not say "one employee becomes two employees"; do not claim 2x productivity or any productivity multiple; no headcount-reduction or FTE language anywhere outward-facing.

### Conservative illustrative floor [SCENARIO — not a forecast or a measured result]

Retained from Revision 6 but **demoted**: it is a deliberately cautious floor, not the headline vision.

**Outward-facing wording:**
> Even at a deliberately cautious **one hour a week**, **1,000 employees** would release around **1,000 hours every week**, or around **50,000 hours a year** assuming 50 working weeks.

- *1,000 employees* is an illustrative scenario size — **not** a confirmed pilot population and **not** a verified AIA employee count.
- *Reasonableness check (may be used outwardly):* 1 hour is 2.5% of a 40-hour week, and about one-eighth of the ~8 hours/week (nearly 20% of 40 hours) McKinsey estimates knowledge workers spend looking for internal information or tracking down colleagues.
- Phase 3 replaces this scenario with a projection built from measured data.

**Back-pocket for Q&A only — not outward-facing:** *Sensitivity:* at 30 minutes/week → ~500 hours/week; at 2 hours/week → ~2,000 hours/week.

**INTERNAL CONTEXT ONLY — do not use in the form, deck or pitch:** at ~2,000 working hours per full-time employee per year, ~50,000 hours ≈ the annual working time of ~25 full-time employees. Excluded outwardly because Riley is not a headcount-reduction idea.

### Link to financial metrics [HYPOTHESIS]

Faster, better-supported back-office work plausibly supports operational efficiency, service quality and speed, which can indirectly feed VONB/ANP economics. To be validated with AIA's TDA methodology, not asserted as a number.

## 10. Pilot plan & timeline (form field 9)

Phases run in sequence; each phase proceeds only if the previous one shows results. **Phase 1 is deliberately narrow:** it uses only the minimum capabilities required to demonstrate one meaningful workflow, not every possible system. This protects feasibility, speed, governance and the budget assumption. The broader hands (Section 0) are the long-term architecture, not the day-one scope.

| Phase | Timing | Milestone / deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 0 — Validate & select | Month 1 | Select the first live pilot team and workflow, weighing business value, implementation readiness, data sensitivity and governance complexity (customer complaints is one candidate; a lower-sensitivity internal workflow may be chosen if it can prove Riley faster and more safely); define the minimum capabilities that workflow needs; baseline effort, completion time, manual steps and completed outputs; confirm data access, security, privacy, audit-record and governance requirements (including feedback governance and any Microsoft 365 access, Section 12); nominate the independent quality reviewer |
| Phase 1 — MVP pilot | Months 2–4 | Deploy Riley for the selected workflow, with one team and only the minimum capabilities required; employee approval before final action; measure Level 1 metrics against the baseline, including independent quality sample reviews. **Go/no-go gate:** proceed only if employee effort is reduced, quality is maintained or improved, and usage is sustained |
| Phase 2 — Extend Riley | Months 5–7 | Extend Riley with another approved capability — an AI tool built by another AIA team is the strongest example — and to a second workflow or team. Test question: *can Riley add another approved capability while keeping the same employee experience and acceptable quality?* **Go/no-go gate:** the new capability is usable through Riley without a new tool for employees, with effort, quality and usage holding up across both workflows. If the chosen capability cannot be connected, select another approved capability and record why the first attempt failed |
| Phase 3 — Scale toward the vision | Months 8–12 | Extend to more back-office teams and connected capabilities; replace the illustrative scenario with a measured projection |

## 12. Governance — designed for an insurer

Conceptual and practical. No specific memory, logging or training architecture is committed to.

1. **Employee's own access only.** Riley acts with the access the requesting employee is already authorized to use and never bypasses existing permissions. This includes Microsoft 365: Riley only reads Outlook, Teams or file content that is available to the requesting employee, and only through approved enterprise controls. **(Revision 7)**
2. **Data stays under AIA controls.** Sensitive and customer data remain subject to AIA's existing data, privacy and information-security controls. External information comes only from approved sources.
3. **Approval before final action.** During the pilot, Riley may plan and execute intermediate steps itself, but the employee reviews and approves the output before anything is finalized, sent to anyone, or entered into a business system. The employee remains accountable.
4. **Traceable and reviewable.** Riley keeps an appropriate record of the work it performs: the employee's request, the systems or capabilities used, the output produced, the employee's approval, and the final action where relevant. Record content, retention and access follow AIA's existing policies and are confirmed in Phase 0.
5. **Governed learning.** What feedback is captured, who can see it, and how improvements are reviewed before they change Riley's behaviour are agreed with risk and IT in Phase 0. The learning mechanism itself is a build decision.
6. **Independent quality checks.** During the pilot, periodic sample reviews by an appropriate business reviewer or subject-matter expert supplement the requesting employee's own review.

## 13. Scalability (form field 14)

- **Three directions of scale:** more employees, more workflows, more connected capabilities (systems, data sources, internal AI tools and other approved services).
- **Near-term:** a large back-office population across many functions is in scope before any cross-market expansion.
- **Mechanism:** each approved capability connected to Riley can become available to employees permitted to use it through the same coworker **[HYPOTHESIS, tested in Phase 2]**.
- **Cross-market:** the core Riley experience can be reused across markets, while each market adds its own system integrations, data controls, governance and process configuration. Not a near-free replication.
- **Vision:** every AIA employee can have access to Riley **[VISION, not a forecast]**.
- **Enduring value:** Section 7.

---

## 14. Feasibility (form field 15)

- Delivered in Microsoft Teams, which employees already use, lowering adoption friction. Trust still has to be earned through reliable results.
- Built on existing or emerging enterprise AI/agent technology, not a from-scratch engine. Platform left open.
- Starts narrow — one team, one workflow, minimum capabilities — with each phase gated on measured results.
- Governance designed in from the start (Section 12), including employee-level access to Microsoft 365 content through approved enterprise controls. The model is **review and approval before final action**.
