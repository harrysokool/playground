# Fragmentation evidence: what to gather before submission

_Internal note for Harry. Not part of the submission. Written 2026-09-23._

## What the repository contains

No independent evidence. The only mentions of separately built AIA AI tools are in the submission's own files (`idea.txt`, `master_story.md`, `application_form_draft.md`). All of them rest on your personal observation. The submission correctly presents it as an observation ("From what I observe…"). Nothing here should be presented as fact until real evidence exists.

## The smallest real evidence that would help most (in priority order)

1. **One real workflow, timed (strongest, about 1 hour of effort).** Pick one piece of work you or a colleague actually do that needs several tools or systems, and write down:
   - the steps
   - which tools or systems are used
   - where outputs are copied or combined by hand
   - roughly how long it takes and how often it happens

   One sentence in the form ("In one real reporting task I timed, an employee used N separate tools and spent about X hours combining their outputs by hand") answers both "is the problem real?" and "is 1 hour a week plausible?".

2. **A count of AI tools employees can already access (about 30 minutes).** From intranets, tool catalogues or announcements you can legitimately see: "Employees in my area can currently access N separate AI tools or assistants." Use a count only, or anonymised descriptions ("a document summarisation tool built by one team"). No names unless they're public internally and you're allowed to cite them.

3. **A small colleague poll (about 1 day, depending on responses).** 10–20 colleagues, three questions:
   - How many AI tools do you use at work?
   - How often do you manually combine outputs from different tools or systems?
   - Would you rather ask one assistant?

   Report the real sample size and the real answers, even if they're mixed.

4. **Two or three anonymised internal AI tools (supporting).** Short descriptions of what separate teams have built, to make slide 3's "AI tool team A/B/C" concrete.

## Rules for using it

- Use only numbers you actually collected. State the sample size and the date.
- Keep "observation" wording if the evidence is small. "In a quick poll of 15 colleagues…" is fine; "employees across AIA…" is not.
- If a finding weakens the story (for example, most people use only one AI tool), tell me and we'll adjust the framing instead of hiding it.
