# Research Protocol

**Protocol version:** 1.2  
**Status:** adopted before historical-pattern analysis  
**Scope:** statistical properties, exact probabilities, strategy evaluation, and ticket economics

This protocol defines the scientific rules of the project before exploratory patterns or strategy
results are examined. Any later amendment must be dated, justified, and recorded in
`docs/decisions/`. An amendment cannot retroactively convert an exploratory result into a
confirmatory one.

## Research objectives

1. Measure how closely historical Mark Six draws resemble draws from the applicable fair lottery
   mechanism.
2. determine whether any information available before a draw produces a reproducible predictive
   advantage over chance on future, unseen draws.
3. Derive exact prize probabilities under each verified historical rule version.
4. Estimate ticket expected value under documented prize, jackpot, rollover, and prize-sharing
   assumptions.
5. Determine whether ticket construction or number popularity can affect expected payout through
   coverage or prize splitting without implying that winning numbers are predictable.
6. Produce reproducible results that expose uncertainty, negative findings, and failed strategies.

## Main research questions

- Is there evidence that future winning-number outcomes can be predicted better than an appropriate
  random baseline?
- Do observed frequencies, combinations, gaps, and draw-to-draw relationships differ materially
  from a fair-draw model?
- What are the exact probabilities for every prize division under each verified rule version?
- What is the expected value of a ticket at different jackpot levels and under different
  prize-sharing assumptions?
- How do rollovers and special jackpots affect expected value?
- Can avoiding commonly selected combinations improve conditional payout by reducing expected prize
  sharing, even though it cannot change the draw probability of a combination?
- Do Multiple and Banker entries differ from buying the equivalent set of ordinary combinations
  after cost, coverage, duplicated outcomes, and covariance are accounted for?
- Given an explicit objective and fixed budget, which ticket construction best serves that objective?
- Do pre-specified historical strategies outperform random selection in walk-forward evaluation?
- Can timing participation around jackpots materially improve long-run expected return?

## Prediction and predictive advantage

A **prediction** is a ticket, ranking, probability distribution, or other score for a future draw
that is generated using only information demonstrably available before that draw's decision cutoff.

A strategy has **predictive advantage** only if it satisfies all of the following:

1. It outperforms a pre-specified random baseline on a pre-specified primary metric.
2. The improvement is measured on unseen, out-of-sample draws.
3. Uncertainty excludes effects too small to meet the pre-specified practical threshold.
4. The result remains credible after accounting for all strategies, windows, features, metrics, and
   hypotheses tried by the project.
5. It is robust across reasonable time periods and rule regimes, or its regime dependence was
   specified before testing.
6. It survives the final untouched holdout when that holdout is opened.

A historical pattern is **not evidence of predictability** until it survives this process. Statistical
significance in a descriptive randomness test is not, by itself, predictive advantage.

## Hypotheses

### Primary null hypothesis

Conditional on the verified rule version, each valid draw is generated by the documented lottery
mechanism, and historical draw outcomes contain no information that improves prediction of future
outcomes over an exchangeable random-ticket baseline with identical ticket count and constraints.

The exact null simulator must sample without replacement according to the applicable rule version.
It must preserve structural facts of the game, such as the distinction between main and Extra
Numbers, rather than treating number indicators as independent Bernoulli trials.

### Alternative hypotheses

Alternatives must be directional and registered before confirmatory evaluation. Candidate families
include:

- marginal number probabilities differ from the fair-draw probabilities;
- pair, triple, positional, gap, or draw-transition behavior differs from its fair-draw distribution;
- a fully specified strategy predicts unseen draws better than its matched random baseline;
- jackpot or rollover state changes ticket expected value under a verified payout model; and
- player number-selection preferences change expected prize sharing for equally likely combinations.

Evidence for one alternative does not establish another. In particular, a payout-sharing advantage
does not establish draw predictability.

## Exploratory and confirmatory analysis

**Exploratory analysis** may search broadly, visualize data, compare windows, and generate new
hypotheses. Its outputs must be labelled exploratory. Any hypothesis or strategy designed after
viewing an outcome is considered trained on that outcome, even if no machine-learning algorithm was
used.

**Confirmatory analysis** evaluates a frozen hypothesis, method, metric, and decision threshold on
data not used to design them. Before a confirmatory run, record at least:

- a unique experiment identifier and code revision;
- the exact training, validation, and test boundaries;
- eligibility rules for draws and missing data;
- the strategy, parameters, features, and random-seed policy;
- primary and secondary metrics;
- null baseline and number of baseline replications;
- statistical test, effect-size threshold, and multiplicity family; and
- all intended subgroup or sensitivity analyses.

Changing any material item after seeing confirmatory results creates a new exploratory analysis and
requires a new untouched evaluation period.

## Out-of-sample testing rules

- Feature construction, parameter estimation, thresholds, and strategy selection may use only the
  training portion available at that step.
- Validation data may select among previously specified candidates but may not be reported as a
  final unbiased performance estimate after selection.
- Test data may be evaluated once for the registered experiment. Repeated inspection turns it into
  training data for future work.
- Preprocessing parameters must be fitted inside each training window.
- Every prediction must be persisted before its outcome is joined, with its generation time, data
  cutoff, code revision, configuration hash, and random seed or seed derivation.
- Performance must be reported over all eligible predicted draws, not only favorable subsets.

## Walk-forward testing rules

For each eligible evaluation draw in chronological order:

1. Set a cutoff immediately before the relevant ticket-purchase decision time.
2. Construct the available dataset using records whose availability timestamp is at or before that
   cutoff.
3. Fit or update the strategy using only that dataset.
4. Generate the pre-specified number of tickets or scores and persist the prediction record.
5. Reveal and join the draw outcome only after prediction persistence.
6. Score the prediction and advance to the next draw.

Expanding and fixed-length rolling windows are separate specifications and must not be substituted
after results are seen. Missing or postponed draws are handled according to pre-registered calendar
rules. No tuning may occur using aggregate results from the walk-forward test segment.

## Random baselines

Each strategy must be compared with strong, matched baselines:

- uniformly random valid combinations;
- the same number of tickets and the same budget as the strategy;
- the same entry constraints and applicable rule version;
- independent seeded replications sufficient to estimate baseline uncertainty; and
- when relevant, a popularity-aware random baseline that separates draw prediction from split-risk
  assumptions.

Common random numbers may be used to reduce comparison variance if the pairing is documented.
Seeds make an experiment reproducible; they must not be searched for favorable results. Results must
not rely on one lucky baseline draw.

## Strategy comparison metrics

The primary predictive metrics will be fixed per experiment before testing. Candidate metrics are:

- mean main-number matches per ordinary combination;
- probability of at least `k` main-number matches for pre-specified `k`;
- proper scoring rules, such as log loss or Brier score, when a strategy emits calibrated
  probabilities; and
- return per HKD staked only when contemporaneous prize and winner data support a valid economic
  calculation.

Secondary diagnostics may include prize-division hit counts, maximum drawdown, variance of return,
calibration, ranking metrics, and coverage overlap. Jackpot wins are extremely rare; realized profit
alone is an unstable predictive metric and must be accompanied by exact or simulated uncertainty.

Strategies will be compared with paired draw-level differences where possible. Reports must include
the number of evaluated draws and tickets, point estimates, confidence intervals, baseline
distribution, practical effect size, and all excluded-draw reasons.

## Multiple-hypothesis testing

Every confirmatory experiment defines its hypothesis family in advance. The project will:

- use family-wise error control, such as Holm's method, for a small set of primary confirmatory
  claims;
- use false-discovery-rate control, such as Benjamini-Hochberg, for larger clearly labelled
  exploratory families;
- report raw and adjusted p-values alongside effect sizes and intervals;
- count alternate windows, filters, parameters, strategies, and metrics as researcher degrees of
  freedom; and
- report the full family, including failed tests, rather than only significant results.

Unregistered post-hoc subgroup findings remain exploratory regardless of adjusted p-values.

## Final untouched holdout

Decision 0002 establishes a **prospective, count-based final holdout**. The holdout is the first 312
eligible draws strictly after 2026-09-13. The first eligible draw identifier and final identifier are
recorded from the official source when known; the endpoint is determined by count, not by a mutable
calendar estimate. At the normal three-draw weekly cadence, 312 draws is about two years.

An eligible holdout draw must be completed, have an official result passing rule-aware validation,
and use a draw mechanism covered by a verified rule version. A postponed draw is included on its
actual draw date. A cancelled or void draw is excluded with an evidence-linked reason. A special
jackpot draw remains eligible for number-prediction metrics only if official evidence shows the
number-drawing mechanism is unchanged; its payout metrics require the applicable verified economic
rules. Missing official records delay completion rather than silently reducing the holdout.

This prospective boundary is necessary because Phase 2 researchers inspected outcome records through
2026-09-12. Those and all earlier outcomes are development history, never final-holdout observations.
The 312-draw size is large enough to estimate ordinary draw-level metrics with meaningfully better
precision than a short percentage split, while leaving the much larger historical record for
development and walk-forward evaluation. It is not sufficient by itself for claims based on rare
First Division wins; those require exact probability/simulation analysis or a longer prospectively
registered period.

Holdout raw bytes may be collected and hash-verified by an integrity-only path, but the normal parser,
notebooks, query views, reports, fixtures, logs, and strategy code must deny access to their outcome
fields. A versioned sealed manifest records draw IDs, raw hashes, eligibility decisions, rule IDs, and
access events without revealing numbers or prizes. Until unsealing, operational reporting is limited
to counts, missingness, validation pass/fail, and rule-continuity alerts.

Phase 3 implements the ordinary development-range guard and strict outcome-free manifest schema.
Any request crossing 2026-09-13 is rejected rather than truncated, making boundary behavior visible
to the operator. The manifest rejects extra fields, including winning numbers, Extra Numbers,
prizes, turnover, and any other unreviewed payload.

The holdout may be opened once only after the strategy, code/configuration hashes, preprocessing,
metrics, effect threshold, multiplicity family, exclusions, and analysis script are frozen. Results
are reported for all eligible draws. A strategy changed after opening has failed the final-holdout
condition and must wait for a new prospective holdout.

If a rule change affects the draw mechanism or a registered metric, accumulation pauses before the
first affected draw. The sealed pre-change observations are not inspected. The team must either
finish under a still-valid registered estimand, pre-register stratified handling supported by both
rule versions, or begin a new 312-draw sequence. The choice is made without opening outcomes and is
recorded by a new decision record.

## Data leakage rules

Leakage includes direct outcomes and any transformed, aggregated, revised, or indirectly encoded
information unavailable at prediction time. Specifically:

- rolling features must exclude the target draw;
- joins must enforce event-time and availability-time cutoffs;
- global normalizers, imputers, feature selectors, and hyperparameters must be fitted inside the
  training window;
- future prize amounts, final winner counts, turnover, jackpot settlements, and revised source data
  cannot be prediction inputs unless published before the cutoff;
- shuffled cross-validation is prohibited for time-dependent evaluation;
- manual inspection of future results counts as information use; and
- test fixtures must not silently contain future aggregates used by production feature code.

Automated tests will later verify cutoff invariants and fail when a feature's maximum source
availability time exceeds the prediction cutoff.

## Time-availability rules

Store separately:

- `event_at`: when the represented draw or event occurred;
- `published_at`: when the source says the information became public, when known;
- `retrieved_at`: when this project obtained it; and
- `as_of_at`: the latest information timestamp allowed for a prediction.

Historical pages may contain corrections or fields published after a draw. Their presence on a page
does not prove pre-draw availability. When publication timing is unknown, the field is ineligible for
prediction unless a conservative, documented rule establishes availability.

All operational times use timezone-aware timestamps. Draw-local dates and times use
`Asia/Hong_Kong`; stored timestamps use UTC.

## Statistical significance and useful predictive power

A small p-value measures incompatibility with a specified null model under its assumptions. It does
not measure effect magnitude, future repeatability, profitability, causal mechanism, or freedom from
selection bias. With many tests or a large sample, negligible deviations can be statistically
significant.

Before confirmatory testing, each claim needs a smallest effect size of practical interest. A useful
predictive advantage must exceed that threshold with uncertainty, survive costs and payout-sharing
assumptions when economics are claimed, and remain stable out of sample. Otherwise it will be
reported as statistically detectable but not practically useful, or as inconclusive.

## Reporting uncertainty

Reports will include, as appropriate:

- confidence or compatibility intervals with their construction method;
- effect sizes in original units and relative to a matched baseline;
- adjusted and unadjusted p-values;
- Monte Carlo standard errors and simulation counts;
- sensitivity to rule versions, missing-data decisions, time windows, and prize-sharing assumptions;
- predictive intervals for estimated economic outcomes; and
- clear separation of sampling uncertainty, model uncertainty, data-quality uncertainty, and
  behavioral assumptions.

Bootstrap methods must respect temporal dependence when present. Simulation results must state the
seed policy and demonstrate adequate convergence for the precision claimed.

## Strategy freezing and amendments

A strategy specification includes its inputs, transformations, lookback windows, parameters,
tie-breaking, ticket count, random behavior, and missing-data policy. It is frozen by configuration
hash and code revision before test outcomes are examined.

Changing a strategy after seeing validation or test results creates a new strategy version. That
version may be evaluated on a later untouched period but cannot inherit the original claim. Bug fixes
that could change predictions also create a new version; reports must show both the defect and its
impact rather than silently replacing results.

## Recording failed strategies

Every executed strategy run receives an immutable `strategy_run_id` and records its specification,
data cutoffs, code/config hashes, outcomes, and status. Negative, null, invalid, and failed runs remain
in the experiment registry. Exclusion requires a reason written without reference to whether the
result was favorable.

Reports will include the denominator of attempted strategies and a failure table. Renaming,
parameter-tweaking, or minor reformulation does not make a previously attempted strategy disappear
from the multiple-testing family.

## Use of simulation

Simulation will be used to:

- obtain null distributions for statistics with inconvenient exact distributions;
- study ticket portfolios, correlated prize outcomes, prize-sharing assumptions, and bankroll risk;
- conduct power analysis before tests; and
- independently validate closed-form calculations.

Simulators must reproduce the verified rule version, use reproducible seeds or recorded random-state
streams, and be tested against exact moments or probabilities where available. Simulation counts are
chosen for required precision, not for obtaining a desired answer.

## Preference for exact probability

When a closed-form combinatorial or probability calculation exists, it is the authoritative method.
Monte Carlo estimates may check the implementation but may not replace the exact result. Exact
calculations must use integer combinatorics or exact rational/decimal arithmetic where practical,
with tested identities such as exhaustive and mutually exclusive prize probabilities summing to one.

## Reproducibility and reporting conduct

- Derived artifacts must identify the raw-source hashes, parser version, code revision, configuration
  hash, and generation time.
- Notebooks are presentation and exploration surfaces, not the sole home of research logic.
- Missing data, repairs, deviations, exclusions, and conflicting sources are reported explicitly.
- No chart may truncate or transform axes in a way that obscures the relevant null variation.
- No result may be described as a winning system. Number suggestions, if ever produced, must be
  separated from predictive claims and accompanied by the evidence status and gambling-risk context.
